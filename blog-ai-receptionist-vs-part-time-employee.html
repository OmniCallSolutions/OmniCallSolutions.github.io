AI Receptionist vs Hiring a Part-Time Employee: Cost Comparison

← Back to all guides

When a business first starts missing calls, the instinct is often "I should hire someone part-time to answer the phone." It's a reasonable first thought — but once you actually run the numbers side by side, the comparison usually isn't close.

The real cost of a part-time receptionist

A part-time employee isn't just their hourly wage. Once you account for the full picture, the real monthly cost usually includes:

  • Hourly wage, typically for 15–25 hours a week
  • Payroll taxes and any mandated benefits
  • Time spent training them on your business, pricing, and common questions
  • Turnover — the same training cost repeats every time someone leaves
  • Coverage gaps during sick days, holidays, and time off

Even at a modest wage, a part-time receptionist covering business hours only (not evenings, weekends, or holidays) commonly costs somewhere in the $800–$1,500+/month range once taxes and training time are factored in — and that's before counting the cost of the calls missed during the hours they're simply not working.

The real cost of an AI receptionist

By comparison, an AI receptionist is priced by call volume, not by hours worked. OmniCall Solutions' plans run $199/month for 300 minutes, $299/month for 700 minutes, or $449/month for 1,200 minutes — a fixed, predictable cost regardless of what time a call comes in.

The coverage difference matters as much as the cost

This is the part a simple wage comparison misses. A part-time employee, even a great one, can only answer calls during the hours they're scheduled. An AI receptionist answers at 2pm and 2am the same way — nights, weekends, and holidays included, without overtime pay or a schedule to manage.

For a business where even a handful of after-hours or weekend calls turn into paying customers, that expanded coverage alone often outweighs the cost difference entirely.

Where a human still wins

This isn't a case for replacing people wholesale. A human is still better at reading emotionally sensitive situations, building a long-term relationship with a regular client, or handling something genuinely unusual that falls outside a normal call pattern. Many businesses land on a mix — AI handles the routine, high-volume, and after-hours calls, while a person handles the situations that actually need a human touch.

The practical takeaway

If the goal is simply making sure the phone always gets answered, at a predictable monthly cost, without a hiring or training cycle — an AI receptionist typically costs less than a part-time hire and covers more hours doing it. If the goal is building an ongoing personal relationship with regular customers, a person still has a role — often alongside an AI receptionist rather than instead of one.
