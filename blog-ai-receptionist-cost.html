How Much Should an AI Receptionist Cost in 2026?

← Back to all guides

Pricing in this space varies a lot, and a good chunk of that variation isn't about quality — it's about how the plan is structured. Here's what actually drives the cost, so you can tell a fair price from an inflated one.

The two things that actually drive price

  • Call minutes included per month — the biggest factor. More minutes, higher base price, because more minutes means more usage of the underlying AI voice technology.
  • How custom the setup is — a simple FAQ-answering agent costs less to build than one handling bookings, orders, and multi-step workflows specific to your business.

Anything beyond those two is mostly just packaging — branding it as "Pro" or "Elite" doesn't change what you're actually paying for underneath.

What to watch out for

A lot of providers in this space hide their pricing entirely behind a "book a call to get a quote" wall. That's not always a red flag on its own, but it does make it hard to compare options quickly, and it's often used to price differently based on how much a business seems willing to pay rather than what the service actually costs to deliver.

It's worth being cautious of long-term contracts with no visible monthly figure anywhere, and of "unlimited minutes" claims — most providers, OmniCall Solutions included, price based on included minutes because that's what the underlying voice AI actually costs to run per minute of conversation.

What a reasonable range looks like

As a general benchmark, a small business getting a genuinely useful volume of call handling should expect to pay somewhere in the range of $150–$500 per month, depending on call volume and how custom the setup is. For reference, OmniCall Solutions' own three plans sit at $199/month for 300 minutes, $299/month for 700 minutes, and $449/month for 1,200 minutes — with fully custom plans available for businesses whose needs don't fit a standard tier.

The real question to ask

Rather than just comparing sticker prices, it's worth asking: how many calls does my business actually get in a month, and how many minutes does that translate to? A plan that looks cheap but only covers a fraction of your real call volume ends up costing more once overage fees are added — while a slightly higher plan that comfortably covers your actual volume is usually the better value.
